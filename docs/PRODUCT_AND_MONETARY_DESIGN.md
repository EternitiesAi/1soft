# A native coin, with an agent-native account around it

Decision draft · 24 September 2026 · Branding and monetary policy remain undecided.

## Product contract

The primary deliverable is an independent Layer 1 and its native asset—not a token on somebody else's chain and not a wallet product substituted for the coin. Its account application should be simple enough for ordinary people, useful to persistent agents, and capable of connecting to existing financial systems through explicitly approved integrations.

The product has three separable layers: the independent protocol; the human/agent account and authorization software; and external payment, custody, conversion and card services. An outage or change in the app must not redefine the public coin's rules. External-provider assurances must not be misrepresented as properties of the chain.

The initial audience hypothesis is creators and small teams whose agents buy research, rendering, storage and other digital services. Lunari can provide a first integration, but external agent runtimes must be able to participate. This is a hypothesis to test, not established product-market fit.

## Two monetary instruments, two different promises

A native dollar unit is a legitimate Layer 1 design. Its outstanding units would represent redemption obligations supported by a real reserve/issuer arrangement. Success would mean dependable redemption and use—not a promised price increase.

A market-priced network coin is a different design. It can serve protocol fees and, if the selected consensus requires it, staking or other network-security functions. No guarantee of price, demand, liquidity, yield or appreciation follows from those functions.

Working recommendation for evaluation: preserve a genuine native network coin and design an independently accounted dollar instrument for ordinary spending. This is not approval to issue a second asset, sell either asset, or choose an allocation. The alternative remains a single native-dollar network if dollar settlement is the final monetary objective. Do not obscure this choice in the branding.

The 100 DEV genesis used by this package is only a test fixture. It is not a public supply, premine, allocation, price or monetary-policy decision. The fixture has no staking, inflation, public minting, redemption, post-genesis account creation or consensus.

## Why the coin would be agent-native

The proposed protocol should understand a responsible principal, a persistent account, current authority, delegated keys, a bounded permission, and a payment intention as distinct objects. A copied model or transcript must not copy the right to spend. A lineage relationship must not silently transfer funds or legal ownership. Ordinary people should be able to use the coin without owning an agent.

The most useful candidate differentiator is economic continuity: the system preserves obligations across restarts and migrations while invalidating obsolete authority. Claims of scientific sentience, legal personhood or patentable novelty are not required and are not made.

The first native model supports signed transfers, recipient-constrained grants, grant nonces, explicit revocation and epoch fencing. It does not yet implement the complete proposed lifecycle. In particular, fencing is not owner-key recovery, full migration, or proof that a particular model is the same resident.

A global budget spanning child grants and payment routes remains an integration requirement. The current native model shares a balance among its grants but does not provide one parent budget across distinct grants. The previous JavaScript budget-tree model is a separate reference, not silently integrated into the new native core.

## Three pools that must stay separate

Redemption backing supports outstanding redeemable dollar liabilities. Company capital pays for the business. Explicit continuity funding purchases preservation and recovery services. The same funds cannot be consumed for compute while still being counted as available redemption backing. A venture investment is not itself proof of reserves.

Account displays must distinguish bank deposits, issuer liabilities, self-custodied crypto, custodied crypto claims and pending obligations. Crypto assets do not gain FDIC insurance merely by being placed beside a bank balance in an app. See the [FDIC explanation](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/financial-products-not-insured).

## The account experience

Start with a person's purpose: connect an agent, explain what it may buy, set a budget, review recovery, and authorize the exact policy through a trusted interface. The agent may recommend a policy; it may not approve its own expansion.

The home screen should show available working funds, held obligations, native holdings, and a small set of understandable controls. Show which provider owes or holds each balance. Do not blend simulated, pending, wrapped and native assets into a single spendable number.

An illustrative permission: “Up to $120 this month, only approved research and rendering providers. Ask before a new merchant, asset or credit obligation. Do not trade or borrow.” Translate this into an enforceable schema; prose alone is not authorization.

Separate stopping new requests, revoking a device, ending a relationship, exporting continuity and closing an account. Past externally accepted payments may remain obligations after revocation. Continuity must never be funded by hidden debits after a person disconnects.

## Design language

Use a recognizable coin mark and a coherent set of denominations, transaction states and protocol terms. Aim for permanence through useful details: legible receipts, clear failure messages, credible recovery, transparent rules and careful typography. The enclosed visual study uses warm mineral backgrounds, restrained green/ink surfaces and an original metallic coin treatment. It is deliberately provisional, not a trademark or final brand selection.

The Verge reference is treated as the founder's experiential preference for a tangible currency and a distinctive technical identity. It is not a direction to copy its branding or assert that privacy technology alone ensures adoption. No promises of complete anonymity or guaranteed market value belong in the design.

## Public launch boundary

No customer funds, private keys, bank credentials or card details belong in the present package. A real launch requires measured network operation, documented validator trust, independent security review, appropriate legal/partner arrangements, incident response, reserve/redemption operations where applicable, and a bounded pilot. Software delivery does not confer a banking charter or issuing permission.
